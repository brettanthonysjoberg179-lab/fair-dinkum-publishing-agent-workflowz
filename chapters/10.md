# Chapter 10: Content Generation Pipeline

*Outline architecture, manuscript writing, editorial review loops, cover design, and ebook assembly.*

---

## The content generation challenge

Generating a 250-page ebook isn't a single prompt — it's a multi-stage process that balances creativity with consistency. The pipeline breaks down into five stages:

1. **Outline** — hierarchical structure with word count targets
2. **Drafting** — chapter-by-chapter manuscript generation
3. **Editorial review** — structural and line-level feedback
4. **Fact-checking** — claim verification and compliance
5. **Production** — final formatting and assembly

Each stage has its own agent, its own validation, and its own retry logic.

## Outline architecture

The Outline Architect creates a three-level hierarchy:

```
Book (240 pages / 60,000 words)
├── Part 1: Foundations (60 pages / 15,000 words)
│   ├── Chapter 1: Why this matters (20 pages / 5,000 words)
│   │   ├── Section 1.1: The problem (5 pages / 1,250 words)
│   │   ├── Section 1.2: The opportunity (5 pages / 1,250 words)
│   │   ├── Section 1.3: The evidence (5 pages / 1,250 words)
│   │   └── Section 1.4: Your action plan (5 pages / 1,250 words)
│   ├── Chapter 2: Core concepts (20 pages / 5,000 words)
│   └── Chapter 3: First steps (20 pages / 5,000 words)
├── Part 2: Implementation (120 pages / 30,000 words)
│   └── ...
└── Part 3: Mastery (60 pages / 15,000 words)
    └── ...
```

Each section has a purpose statement, a word count target, and cross-references to related sections. The outline isn't just a table of contents — it's a detailed brief for each writing assignment.

### Balancing the outline

The architect enforces three balance rules:

1. **Part balance:** No part exceeds 150% of any other part's word count.
2. **Chapter balance:** Chapters within a part stay within 80-120% of the average chapter length for that part.
3. **Pacing balance:** Action-oriented chapters alternate with concept-heavy chapters.

These rules prevent the common failure mode where the first half of the book is meticulous and the second half is rushed.

## Manuscript generation

The Manuscript Author takes the outline and generates the actual content, one chapter at a time. It uses a sliding context window approach:

### Chapter-level context

For each chapter, the author receives:

- The complete outline (for overall structure)
- The previous chapter's closing paragraphs (for voice continuity)
- The current chapter's section breakdown (for detailed guidance)
- All positioning and audience context (for tone consistency)

This context window is large enough for coherence but small enough to fit within token limits.

### Voice consistency

Maintaining a consistent voice across 60,000 words is harder than generating any single 5,000-word chapter. The author uses three techniques:

1. **Style guide:** A concrete document that defines voice attributes (sentence length range, vocabulary level, humour frequency, formality level).
2. **Anchor passages:** Three exemplar passages written during outline approval that define the target voice.
3. **Recap prompts:** Each chapter prompt includes the closing paragraph of the previous chapter, grounding the voice in what came immediately before.

### Handling facts and citations

The author doesn't make up statistics. Every factual claim is either:

- Sourced from the research artifacts stored in the database
- Flagged as `[CITATION NEEDED]` for the Fact-Check Agent to resolve
- Presented as common knowledge that doesn't require citation

This keeps the author focused on prose and structure, leaving verification to the specialist.

## Editorial review

The Editorial Agent reviews the manuscript in two passes:

### Pass 1: Structural

The editor evaluates each chapter against its outline brief:

- Does the chapter fulfill its stated purpose?
- Is the section ordering logical?
- Are there gaps where promised content is missing?
- Is there redundancy with other chapters?

Structural issues are returned as revision requests with specific section references.

### Pass 2: Line-level

The editor reviews for:

- Sentence-level clarity and flow
- Grammar and punctuation
- Consistent terminology (using a project glossary)
- Active vs passive voice ratio
- Paragraph length variation

Line-level issues are returned as annotated suggestions, not rewrites. The author decides which suggestions to implement.

### The revision loop

If either pass finds significant issues, the chapter enters a revision loop:

```
Draft → Structural review → Revision → Line-level review → Final
```

The loop runs a maximum of three iterations. If the chapter still doesn't meet quality thresholds after three rounds, it's flagged for human review. In practice, most chapters converge on the first or second iteration.

## Fact-checking and compliance

The Fact-Check Agent verifies three categories:

### 1. Factual claims

Every statistic, date, name, and claim is checked against the research database. Claims without a supporting source are flagged with `[UNVERIFIED]`. The agent attempts to find sources; if it can't, the claim is either removed or attributed as "industry estimate."

### 2. Copyright and attribution

The agent checks all quotes, images, and data tables against copyright databases. Fair use is assessed on four factors: purpose, nature, amount, and market effect. Anything borderline is flagged for legal review.

### 3. Compliance

Australian-specific compliance checks:

- **Australian Consumer Law:** No misleading claims, no false testimonials
- **Defamation:** No identifiable individuals portrayed negatively without evidence
- **Financial advice:** No specific investment recommendations without appropriate disclaimers
- **Health advice:** No specific medical claims without evidence and disclaimers

Compliance failures block publication until resolved.

## Cover design

The Cover Creative Director produces a cover brief, not the final image. The brief includes:

### Visual direction

- **Mood board:** Three to five reference images that capture the target aesthetic
- **Colour palette:** Primary, secondary, and accent colours with hex codes
- **Composition sketch:** Description of layout — image placement, title position, author name placement
- **Typography:** Font pairings for title, subtitle, and author name with size ratios

### Image prompts

For each surface (front cover, spine, back cover), the director produces a detailed image generation prompt:

```
Front cover prompt: "A sunlit Australian native garden with flowering 
grevilleas and bottlebrushes, shallow depth of field, golden hour lighting, 
warm earth tones, professional garden photography style, 4:5 aspect ratio, 
no text"
```

### Production-ready assets

The final cover is assembled from the generated image assets, typography, and barcode/ISBN block. The Production Agent handles this assembly.

## Ebook production

The Ebook Production Agent takes the final manuscript and cover assets and produces the deliverable files:

### EPUB generation

1. Convert markdown to XHTML with semantic markup
2. Apply CSS styles for typography and layout
3. Embed cover image and metadata (title, author, ISBN, publisher)
4. Generate table of contents (NCX and HTML)
5. Validate against EPUB3 specification

### PDF generation

1. Convert markdown to PDF via print-ready styles
2. Apply page layout (margins, headers, footers, page numbers)
3. Embed fonts and images at print resolution (300dpi)
4. Add cover as separate page
5. Generate PDF/UA tags for accessibility

### Quality validation

Both formats are validated:

- **EPUB:** EPUBCheck validation, structure verification, link checking
- **PDF:** PDF/A compliance, font embedding, image resolution, accessibility tags

Any validation errors are reported with specific line numbers or element references for quick correction.

## Key takeaways

- Outline is a detailed brief, not just a table of contents.
- Manuscript generation uses chapter-level context windows for voice consistency.
- Editorial review is two-pass: structural, then line-level.
- Fact-checking covers facts, copyright, and compliance.
- Cover design produces a brief and prompts, not final images.
- EPUB and PDF production includes validation against industry standards.
